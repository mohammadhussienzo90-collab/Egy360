Egypt Travel FAQ

Everything you need to know before your Egyptian adventure

Visa & Entry Requirements

Do I need a visa to visit Egypt?

Most nationalities can obtain a visa on arrival at Egyptian airports for $25 USD, valid for 30 days. Citizens of the EU, US, UK, Canada, Australia, Japan, and many other countries are eligible.

You can also apply for an e-visa online before travel, which can be convenient for faster immigration processing.

Documents needed: Valid passport (6+ months validity), return ticket, hotel booking confirmation, and cash for visa fee.

How long can I stay in Egypt?

The standard tourist visa allows a 30-day stay. Extensions are possible at the Mogamma building in Cairo's Tahrir Square or at immigration offices in major cities.

Extensions typically cost around 50-100 EGP and can add 30 more days. Overstaying can result in fines when departing.

Best Time to Visit

What is the best time to visit Egypt?

October to April is the ideal time to visit Egypt when temperatures are comfortable (20-25°C / 68-77°F).

Peak season (December-February): Most pleasant weather, but higher prices and more crowds at popular sites.

Shoulder season (October-November, March-April): Great weather with fewer tourists and better deals.

Summer (May-September): Very hot, especially in Upper Egypt (40°C+). Good for Red Sea beach resorts with lower prices.

Is Ramadan a good time to visit Egypt?

Ramadan offers a unique cultural experience, but be aware that many restaurants close during the day. Tourist sites remain open, and hotels cater to visitors.

Evenings during Ramadan are magical, with Iftar feasts and festive atmospheres. It's a wonderful time to experience authentic Egyptian hospitality.

Safety & Health

Is Egypt safe for tourists?

Yes, Egypt is generally safe for tourists. Major tourist areas including Cairo, Giza, Luxor, Aswan, and Red Sea resorts have a strong security presence.

Tips for safe travel:

  • Use reputable tour operators and licensed guides
  • Stay in well-reviewed hotels
  • Avoid isolated areas at night
  • Keep valuables secure and be aware of pickpockets
  • Check travel advisories before your trip

We recommend booking tours through verified operators for peace of mind.

Can I drink tap water in Egypt?

No, do not drink tap water. Bottled water is inexpensive (about 5-10 EGP) and widely available everywhere.

Use bottled water for drinking and brushing teeth. Ice in reputable restaurants and hotels is typically made from purified water.

Bring basic medication for stomach upsets just in case, and avoid raw vegetables washed in tap water at street food stalls.

What vaccinations do I need for Egypt?

No vaccinations are legally required to enter Egypt, but the following are recommended:

  • Hepatitis A and B
  • Typhoid
  • Tetanus-diphtheria
  • Routine vaccinations (MMR, flu)

Consult your doctor or a travel clinic 4-6 weeks before departure. Bring prescription medications with original labels.

Money & Costs

What currency is used in Egypt?

The Egyptian Pound (EGP or LE) is the official currency. US Dollars and Euros are also widely accepted at hotels, tour companies, and tourist shops.

Exchange rates: As of 2024, approximately 1 USD = 30-50 EGP (rates fluctuate).

Tips:

  • ATMs are available in major cities and tourist areas
  • Credit cards accepted at hotels and upscale establishments
  • Carry small bills for tipping and small purchases
  • Exchange at banks for better rates than hotels
How much should I budget for Egypt?

Budget travel: $30-50/day (hostels, local food, public transport)

Mid-range: $80-150/day (3-4 star hotels, guided tours, nice restaurants)

Luxury: $200+/day (5-star hotels, private guides, fine dining)

Major costs include accommodation, tour guides, entrance fees (Pyramids: ~400 EGP), and internal flights if traveling long distances.

How much should I tip in Egypt?

Tipping (baksheesh) is an important part of Egyptian culture. Guidelines:

  • Restaurants: 10-15% of the bill
  • Tour guides: 50-100 EGP per person per day
  • Drivers: 30-50 EGP per day
  • Hotel staff: 10-20 EGP per service
  • Bathroom attendants: 5 EGP

Keep small bills handy for tipping throughout your trip.

Getting Around

How do I get from Cairo to Luxor?

By Flight (recommended): 1 hour, from $50 one-way. EgyptAir and Air Cairo operate multiple daily flights.

By Sleeper Train: 10 hours overnight, $80-100 for a private cabin. A comfortable and scenic way to travel.

By Bus: 8-10 hours, from $15. GoBus offers comfortable coaches with WiFi.

By Private Car: 8-10 hours, $150-200 with driver. Flexible but long.

Book flights and trains in advance during peak season. Compare transport options here.

Is Uber available in Egypt?

Yes! Uber and Careem (local competitor) are available in Cairo and Alexandria. They're safe, affordable, and avoid the hassle of negotiating with taxi drivers.

Payment can be by card or cash. Prices are typically 50-70% lower than tourist taxis.

What to Pack & Wear

What should I wear when visiting Egypt?

Egypt is a relatively conservative country. Dress modestly to show respect and avoid unwanted attention:

For women:

  • Cover shoulders and knees in public
  • Bring a scarf to cover head when entering mosques
  • Loose, breathable clothing is best

For men:

  • Avoid shorts outside resort areas
  • Collared shirts preferred at upscale restaurants

General tips: Comfortable walking shoes, sun hat, sunglasses, and sunscreen are essential.

Trip Planning

How many days do I need to see Egypt?

A minimum of 7-10 days is recommended to see Egypt's highlights:

  • Cairo & Giza: 2-3 days (Pyramids, Sphinx, Egyptian Museum, Khan el-Khalili)
  • Luxor: 2-3 days (Valley of the Kings, Karnak Temple, Luxor Temple)
  • Aswan: 1-2 days (Philae Temple, Nubian villages, Abu Simbel day trip)
  • Nile Cruise: 3-4 days (Luxor to Aswan or vice versa)
  • Red Sea: 2-3 days (optional - Hurghada, Sharm El Sheikh)

For a comprehensive experience including Alexandria and the desert, plan for 14 days.
